Paint bubbling or the wall feels soft?

Both mean water is in the wall. Drywall is paper-faced gypsum that soaks and softens, and insulation and framing behind it stay wet long after the surface looks dry. Wall and drywall water damage in Colerain, OH starts with finding how far the moisture goes, so call (740) 231-9132 and describe the stain.

Signs the wall has been wet

  • Brown or yellow stains, often with a darker ring at the edge.
  • Peeling, bubbling or cracked paint and wallpaper.
  • Soft, crumbly or swollen drywall, especially at the bottom of the wall.
  • A musty smell with no visible source.
  • Baseboards that pull away or warp.

What happens to wet drywall

Clean-water losses caught early can sometimes be dried in place. Drywall that sagged, crumbled or sat in contaminated water is cut out. Crews usually cut a flood cut, which is a horizontal cut about two feet above the floor, so insulation and the wall cavity can be reached and dried. Meters check the studs and the bottom plate before any new board goes up.

Do not paint over a stain

Painting seals moisture in and hides the problem, which can lead to mold and bubbling again. Stain-blocking primer does not dry a wall. Find the source, dry the cavity and then repair.

How wall cavities are dried

Insulation holds water like a sponge, and fiberglass batts may be removed. Small openings let dry air in, and some jobs use cavity drying systems that push air through. Dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air so it does not settle elsewhere. The structural drying page describes how readings guide the work.

Walls near floors may also involve hardwood or carpet, and ceiling stains are covered on the ceiling page.

Rebuilding the wall

Dry

Studs and bottom plate reach target moisture levels.

Patch

New drywall is hung and taped.

Finish

Joints are sanded and texture is matched.

Paint

Primer and paint blend the repair into the wall.

Matching texture and paint takes skill. Older homes may have plaster, which is repaired differently.

Lead paint caution for older homes

If your home was built before 1978, painted surfaces may contain lead. Cutting, sanding or tearing out old paint creates dust that is hazardous, especially to children and pregnant women. The EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule requires lead-safe practices from firms working on pre-1978 homes. Tell the dispatcher the age of your house when you call.

Insurance and walls

Covered leaks usually include drywall and paint repair. Ask your insurer whether the payout includes painting the whole wall or only the patch, and document the wall before cutting. A slow leak that ran for months may be treated differently from a sudden one.

Bathrooms, exterior walls and shared walls

Walls beside showers and tubs stay wet because the leak is slow and sealed. Exterior walls collect water from roof edges, window flashing and siding gaps, and insulation hides the problem. Shared walls between units carry water from a neighbor, which involves two policies and a landlord.

Each location changes where the crew opens the wall and how it dries the cavity. Tell the dispatcher which wall it is, what room is on the other side and what plumbing runs inside.

How to tell if the leak is still active

A stain that keeps growing, a bubble that refills after you pop it or a damp smell that returns after drying all mean water is still coming. Mark the edge of a stain with a pencil and check it each day. If it moves, a plumber or roofer needs to find the source before repairs.

Standards behind this page

Cutting and drying wall cavities follows ANSI/IICRC S500 principles, and tearing out old paint in a pre-1978 home falls under the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule for lead-safe work practices.

Safety steps before the crew arrives

  • Do not cut into a wall that may have wiring inside until the power is off.
  • Keep children and pregnant women away from dust if the house was built before 1978.
  • Do not paint over a stain.
  • Mark the edge of the stain with a pencil and note the date.
FAQ

Questions about wall and drywall water damage

Can wet drywall be dried instead of replaced?

Sometimes, if the water was clean and caught quickly. Crumbly, sagging or contaminated drywall is replaced.

What is a flood cut?

A horizontal cut in the drywall a couple of feet above the floor, so the cavity behind can be inspected and dried.

Will you match the paint?

The repair includes primer and paint, with colors matched as closely as possible. Whole-wall painting avoids visible patches.

Do you repair plaster walls?

Plaster is handled differently from drywall and dries more slowly. Mention it when you call so the right crew is sent.

What about the ceiling above the wall?

If the stain climbs, water is probably coming from above. See the ceiling page for sagging and safety.
