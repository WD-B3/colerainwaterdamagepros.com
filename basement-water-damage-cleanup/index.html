The below-grade room is where water ends up when a house cannot get rid of it. Basement water damage in Colerain, OH can start with a sump pump that quits in a storm, a wall that seeps after days of rain, a floor drain that pushes water up or a pipe that fails upstairs and runs downhill. Whatever the source, the 24/7 line at (740) 231-9132 sends crews to pump, strip and dry.

When a wet basement is an emergency

Call right away when water is rising, when it touches the furnace, water heater, electrical panel or outlets, or when it came up through a drain. Basement water is also heavy. A half inch across a 400 square foot floor weighs more than 1,000 pounds.

Do not wade in to check the panel

If the electrical panel is in the wet basement, stay out and call the utility or 911. Do not enter a flooded basement to flip a breaker. If the water came from a drain, treat it as sewage and keep children and pets away.

If the ground outside is saturated, ask the crew before the basement is pumped out quickly, since the walls may be holding back groundwater pressure.

Four ways water gets into a basement

Sump pump failure

Pumps fail from power loss, a stuck float, a clogged line or a tired motor, and they tend to fail during the storm that needs them.

Wall and floor seepage

Water pressed against foundation walls finds the joint where the floor meets the wall, or cracks in block and concrete.

Drain and sewer backup

Floor drains and toilets are the low points on the sewer line. See sewage backup cleanup if the water smells.

Plumbing and appliance leaks

Supply lines, water heaters and washers above or beside the basement send water to the lowest level.

Restoration is not waterproofing

Cleaning up and drying a basement restores what the water damaged. It does not stop water from returning. If water enters at the same spot every storm, you need a drainage or foundation fix from a different trade, such as a waterproofing contractor or a grading and gutter correction.

We would rather say so plainly. Drying a room that floods again next month wastes your money. If the cause is a failed sump pump, replacing the pump and adding a battery backup is a plumber's or sump contractor's job.

After the water is out

Pumping gets the standing water out. Drying gets the moisture out of block walls, wood framing and concrete, and that takes air movers and dehumidifiers and daily readings. The structural drying page explains the equipment and the targets, and carpet and pad covers the floor covering.

A basement that stays above about 60 percent relative humidity invites mold. The EPA advises keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent.

Insurance and basement water

Coverage depends on how the water got in. Water from a sudden pipe failure is often covered. Water backing up through a drain or sump is commonly an optional add-on, and rising groundwater or creek water is typically a flood loss that needs separate flood coverage, per the Ohio Department of Insurance. Read your policy and ask your insurer before you decide.

Colerain Township sits in creek country, where Wheeling Creek runs through Blaine and Lansing. Standards behind this page

Under ANSI/IICRC S500, water that rises from a creek or river is Category 3, while clean supply water is Category 1. Which category your basement water falls into decides what is removed and what is dried, so say where the water came from when you call.

FAQ

Questions about basement water damage

Do you handle basement flooding emergencies at night or on weekends in Colerain?

Yes, and storms are when the calls come. The dispatch line stays open around the clock, seven days a week. Tell the dispatcher how deep the water is and what is in the basement.

Should I pump the basement out myself?

A small amount, yes, if no electrical hazard is present. For deep water or saturated ground outside, wait and ask. Pumping too fast can add stress to walls, and wet equipment can shock.

Will you waterproof the basement?

No. Restoration removes water that already got in and repairs the damage. Waterproofing is a separate trade, and the right fix depends on where the water enters.

Is my finished basement ceiling safe after an upstairs leak?

Not necessarily. A ceiling that holds water can sag and fail. See the ceiling water damage page for signs and safe steps.

What if my sump pump failed during a storm?

Get the water out, dry the space and have the pump checked. A battery backup or second pump reduces the chance of a repeat. A plumber or sump contractor handles that part.
