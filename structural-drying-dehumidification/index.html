Walls feel damp and the house smells musty days after a leak?

That feeling means the water is still in the building, even if the puddle is gone. Wet drywall, framing, insulation and subfloors keep releasing moisture for days, and the smell is the first sign of trouble. The fix has a trade name, which is structural drying and dehumidification.

It is the part of water mitigation that happens after water is pumped out. Call (740) 231-9132 any time if your house still feels wet and you are not sure why.

What structural drying actually does

Structural drying removes moisture from the building materials themselves. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air, and meters track the results until the materials reach their dry standard.

Opening windows or running a household fan does not do the same job. Outdoor air in humid weather can add moisture, and without measurement you cannot tell when a wall is actually dry.

The tools and what each one does

ToolPurpose
Moisture meter.Reads moisture inside drywall, wood and concrete without cutting them open.
Thermal imaging camera.Shows cool, wet areas behind surfaces so the wet zone can be mapped.
Air movers.Move air across wet surfaces to speed evaporation.
LGR dehumidifiers.Pull large amounts of moisture out of the air in a closed space.
Desiccant dehumidifiers.Work in cold areas where refrigerant units struggle.
Cavity drying systems.Push dry air into wall cavities through small openings.

Water mitigation and moisture mapping

Mitigation means stopping further loss. In practice it covers shutting off the source, removing standing water, pulling out materials that cannot dry and setting up equipment. The full job is described on the water extraction page.

Moisture mapping is the survey that guides it all. Crews take readings in a grid across floors and walls, mark the wet boundaries and repeat the readings each visit. The map shows progress and tells you when equipment can come out.

How the IICRC classes describe a job

ClassWhat it meansTypical example
Class 1.Least water absorbed, with little wet material.A small spill on a concrete floor.
Class 2.Water reached carpet and walls up to about two feet.A washer leak into a carpeted room.
Class 3.Water came from above and soaked ceilings, walls and floors.A roof leak running down inside a house.
Class 4.Deep saturation in low-porosity materials.Hardwood and plaster that need specialty drying.

These classes come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard and help set the equipment plan. Ask any company whether its crews work to S500.

How long drying takes

There is no honest single number. Carpet and thin drywall can dry in a few days, while hardwood, plaster and framing against a cold wall can take longer. Drying is complete when meter readings match the dry reference for each material, not when a calendar date arrives.

The EPA advises keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent, and that is the same range a dehumidifier targets. See the wall and drywall page for cavities and the hardwood floor page for slow-drying floors.

Insurance and drying documentation

Insurers often want proof that drying worked. Moisture readings, equipment logs and photos create that record. Ask your insurer what it requires before the equipment comes out.

Safety steps while equipment runs

  • Keep power cords out of standing water and out of walkways.
  • Do not move or unplug equipment without calling the crew.
  • Keep children and pets away from fans and hot dehumidifier exhaust.
  • Keep windows and exterior doors closed in the drying area so outdoor humidity stays out.
FAQ

Questions about structural drying

Can I rent air movers and a dehumidifier instead?

Equipment rental is not part of this service. Crews set and monitor the equipment because placement and readings determine how fast structure dries. Machines in the wrong spots leave wet framing behind.

How loud are the machines, and can I stay home?

They run constantly and make noise, and most people sleep elsewhere or tolerate it for a few days. You can usually stay in the house unless the water was contaminated or the job covers the bedrooms.

What is water mitigation?

It is the emergency stage that stops water from spreading and starts the drying. Restoration is the repair afterward. The two stages blend on most jobs.

Is a damp smell always mold?

No, but it is a warning that materials are staying wet. Keep drying them, keep humidity down and ask a professional to look if staining or growth appears.

How do I know drying is finished?

Readings on walls and floors match dry reference materials and the humidity in the space is in the target range. A good crew shows you the numbers.
