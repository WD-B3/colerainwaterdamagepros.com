People search for radon water mitigation systems because two different problems share one word. Water mitigation means drying a building after a leak or flood. Radon mitigation means reducing a radioactive gas that seeps up from the ground. This company does the first. We do not test for radon or install radon systems.

This page explains the difference so you can find the right professional. It is general information, not a substitute for advice from a licensed radon contractor or your health department.

What radon is

Radon is a naturally occurring radioactive gas that forms when uranium in rock and soil breaks down. It has no color, smell or taste. It moves through soil and can enter a building through cracks in a foundation, floor drains, sump openings and gaps around pipes. The U.S. EPA recommends fixing a home if the long-term radon level is at or above 4 picocuries per liter of air.

Radon is a health concern because long exposure raises lung cancer risk. The only way to know your level is to test.

Radon in air and radon in water

PathwayHow it worksTypical fix
Radon in indoor air.Soil gas enters through the foundation and builds up inside.A mitigation system that vents soil gas outside, often by sub-slab depressurization.
Radon in well water.Groundwater carries dissolved radon and releases it when you run the water.Treatment that removes radon from the water, such as aeration or carbon filtration.

In most homes, soil gas in the air is the larger concern. Radon in water matters mostly for private wells, because many public supplies lose most radon before it reaches the tap. EPA has proposed but not finalized a national limit for radon in drinking water, so test your well and ask a qualified professional about results.

Where water damage and radon overlap

The two problems connect in a few ways. A sump pit that collects groundwater also gives radon a path into the basement. A wet crawl space may be sealed with a vapor barrier that also reduces soil gas. A basement that floods may have cracks that let gas in. Fixing the water does not fix the radon, though, and sealing alone is not an accepted radon fix.

If you dry out a basement and also have a radon concern, handle them as two jobs. The basement page explains the water side, and the crawl space page explains the space under the floor.

Who installs radon systems in Ohio

Ohio licenses radon professionals. The state's rules for radon testing and mitigation are in Ohio Administrative Code chapter 3701-69, which sets license procedures for radon mitigation specialists and radon mitigation contractors and standards of conduct for them. Hire someone licensed by the state, ask to see the license and ask which standard the system is designed to meet.

The Ohio Department of Health runs the state radon program and can point you to licensed testers and contractors. A water treatment company or well driller may handle radon in water, so ask them whether they have experience with it.

How testing works

Test

Short-term kits sit in the lowest lived-in level for a few days.

Compare

Results at or above 4 pCi/L call for action under EPA guidance.

Confirm

A follow-up test or longer test checks the number.

If a home has a private well, ask a certified lab how to test the water. Do not guess from the air result.

A sensible order of operations

If your basement is wet and you also plan a radon system, dry first. A radon contractor will need clean access to the slab, and a system installed over a wet floor can trap moisture. Ask the contractor how the system handles sump pits and floor drains. Then keep the area dry. The structural drying page explains the targets.

Questions to ask a radon contractor

  1. Are you licensed by the state, and may I see the license?
  2. What standard will the system follow?
  3. How will you handle the sump pit and floor drains?
  4. What is the plan for retesting after installation?
  5. Does the quote include electrical work for the fan?

Safety steps if you suspect radon

  • Test the lowest lived-in level with a kit or a licensed tester.
  • Do not seal cracks and assume the problem is solved.
  • Hire a radon contractor licensed by the state.
  • Retest after any system is installed.
FAQ

Questions about radon and water

Do you install radon mitigation systems?

No. Radon systems are installed by radon contractors licensed by the state. This company handles water damage drying and cleanup.

Is radon the same as water mitigation?

No. Radon mitigation reduces a gas in the air. Water mitigation dries a building after a leak or flood.

Does a wet basement mean I have radon?

Not necessarily. Moisture and radon are separate. Test your air, regardless of how wet the basement is.

Can sealing cracks fix radon?

Sealing alone is not an accepted radon fix. Licensed contractors use venting systems and may seal as a secondary step.

Where do I test in Colerain Township?

Test the lowest lived-in level of your home with a kit or a licensed tester. The Ohio Department of Health can point you to resources.
