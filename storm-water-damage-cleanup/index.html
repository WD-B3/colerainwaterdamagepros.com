Storm water damage happens when wind and rain break through a roof, wall, window or door and soak what is inside. It differs from a flood, where water rises from outside, and from a plain roof leak, which has its own section on the ceiling page. Storm damage cleanup in Colerain, OH starts with the 24/7 line at (740) 231-9132.

After a storm: what is an emergency

Call immediately for a hole in the roof, windows blown in, siding torn off, a fallen limb that opened the house to rain or water coming through walls or ceilings. Rain that keeps falling keeps soaking.

Safety around storm damage

Stay away from downed power lines and call the utility or 911. Do not enter a room with a sagging ceiling or water at outlets. Wait for an engineer or roofer to confirm a damaged structure is safe.

Cover openings with tarps or plywood if you can do so safely from the ground, and photograph the damage before and after.

Storm or flood? It matters for insurance

Wind-driven rain through a damaged roof or window is generally handled differently from rising water, which is typically excluded unless you carry separate flood coverage. The Ohio Department of Insurance notes that flood insurance is typically not part of homeowners policies. How your loss is classified can change your claim, so describe it exactly and keep photos.

The department also runs a severe weather toolkit that warns about contractor fraud after storms. Be wary of anyone who asks for large payment up front or pressures you to sign over your claim.

Storms in Colerain Township

Heavy rain is the pattern that shows up in the local record. The 1990 Pipe Creek and Wegee Creek flood in Belmont County came from violent thunderstorms, as the U.S. Geological Survey documented, and the county reported flooding again in April 2024. Take storm warnings seriously, and make photos of your property in good condition now so you can show a change later.

Protecting what is not yet wet

While you wait for the crew, move furniture, rugs and electronics away from wet ceilings and walls. Place buckets under active drips, and poke a small hole in a bulging ceiling only if a professional tells you to, since a ceiling heavy with water can drop suddenly.

Keep photos of anything you move and where it came from. Lift rugs off wet floors so dye does not bleed into the surface. Turn off power to circuits near wet areas if you can reach the panel safely. If you cannot, leave it and tell the dispatcher.

Standards behind this page

ANSI/IICRC S500 treats rain that comes through a roof as water that picks up contamination from insulation and building materials, so even storm water is not always handled as clean. The Ohio Department of Insurance publishes a severe weather toolkit that covers claim filing and contractor fraud.

FAQ

Questions about storm water damage

Do you handle storm damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Colerain?

Yes. Storms do not respect business hours, so the dispatch line is open around the clock, every day of the year.

Do you fix the roof?

Roof repair is a roofer's work. This service removes the water that came inside, dries the building and repairs the interior. Tell the dispatcher about any tarps already on.

What is the difference between storm damage and flood damage?

Storm damage means rain or wind opened the building. Flood means water rose from outside. The two are handled differently by insurers, so describe yours exactly.

Should I accept help from a door-to-door contractor after a storm?

Be careful. The Ohio Department of Insurance warns about contractor fraud after storms. Ask for ID, do not pay large sums up front and check with your insurer.

How do I document storm damage?

Take photos and video of every wet area, the roof if safe and any broken windows, and keep receipts for emergency repairs.
