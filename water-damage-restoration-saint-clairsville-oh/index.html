A county seat with shops, colleges and offices

Interstate 70 and US 40 run through town, with US 40 roughly following the old National Road. State Route 9 also serves the city. Ohio Valley Mall opened in 1978 and anchors the main shopping area, while Belmont College and the Ohio University Eastern Campus add institutional buildings.

That mix brings commercial calls as well as home calls. A burst line in a storefront, a roof leak over stock or a failed water heater in an office can close a business. See commercial water damage restoration for how jobs are staged.

Homes on high ground

The city sits at about 1,224 feet, higher than the river towns, so the main water damage worries are roofs, plumbing and storm runoff rather than river flooding. Newer subdivisions and older downtown houses behave differently, and the Clarendon Hotel, built in 1890, is a reminder that old buildings are part of the picture.

The county floodplain office

Belmont County's Commissioners sit at 101 West Main Street in Saint Clairsville, and the county has a floodplain administrator who handles flood hazard area permits. Fees for 2025 were $100 for structural work and $50 for non-structural work. If your property is in a mapped flood hazard area, ask the administrator before you rebuild.

Source: Belmont County Commissioners, floodplain page.

Insurance and storm claims

For storm damage, the Ohio Department of Insurance offers a severe weather toolkit and warns about contractor fraud after storms. Document everything, avoid large up-front payments and call your insurer before work starts. The storm damage page explains how storm losses are handled.

Commercial and campus buildings

The Ohio Valley Mall area, Belmont College and the Ohio University Eastern Campus all involve larger buildings with roofs, mechanical rooms and sprinklers. Failure of a sprinkler line or rooftop drain can flood a large floor area quickly. Building managers should know the location of shutoff valves and keep a contact sheet for emergencies. A short written plan that names who closes which valve saves minutes when a line lets go after hours.

When a manager calls, the dispatcher asks about floor area, flooring, and which rooms must reopen first. Phased drying lets parts of the building return to use while other parts stay closed.

Homes along I-70 and National Road

The newer neighborhoods have slab or basement foundations, finished lower levels and appliances on upper floors. A washer hose or water heater failure on the second floor can soak the ceiling, walls and carpet below. See the water heater page and the appliance leak page for the usual causes.

Older homes near downtown have plaster, wood floors and long-used plumbing. Both types are handled the same way at the start, with water out first and meter readings guiding the rest.
