Martins Ferry is the largest city in Belmont County, with 6,260 residents at the 2020 census, and it lies directly across the Ohio River from Wheeling, West Virginia. A city built on two river plateaus

The town sits on two plateaus between a hill and the river. The lower one holds an industrial park, the football stadium and State Route 7, while the higher plateau is mostly residential and commercial. Elevation is about 761 feet.

That layout matters when water gets in. Homes on the lower bench are closer to the river and to storm runoff, and houses on the slope behind them can see water run downhill into basements. Older homes from the early twentieth century, many built when the city peaked at 14,729 residents in 1940, often have stone or block basements and plaster walls that dry slowly.

Local water supply in Martins Ferry

The city runs its own water treatment plant, completed in 1997, which draws groundwater from an aquifer that flows south from northern Ohio and treats it with filtration and ozonation. The system serves more than 4,000 connections. Bulk supply also goes to neighboring water systems, so a main break can affect more than one customer group. If water is coming from the street or a main, call the city before you call us.

Insurance and floodplain notes

Most of Martins Ferry is in Belmont County, whose floodplain administrator handles flood hazard permits. Homeowners policies usually do not cover rising river or creek water, per the Ohio Department of Insurance, so check for separate flood coverage. After the April 2024 floods, Belmont County's emergency management office reported about $15.7 million in preliminary public damage. Keep photos and receipts and ask your insurer what it needs before work starts.
