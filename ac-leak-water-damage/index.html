Water near the air handler, or a brown stain on the ceiling below it?

Those are classic signs of an AC leak. Air conditioners remove moisture from the air as they cool it, and that water, called condensate, normally drains away through a small line. When the line clogs, freezes or cracks, or the drain pan rusts through, the water goes where gravity sends it.

AC leak water damage in Colerain, OH can ruin a ceiling, a closet floor or insulation before anyone notices. Call (740) 231-9132 if you see dripping, stains or a musty smell near the equipment.

Why air conditioners leak

CauseWhat it looks like
Clogged condensate line.Water backs up in the pan and overflows near the air handler.
Rusted or cracked drain pan.Slow drips beneath the unit, often in attics and closets.
Frozen evaporator coil.Ice on the coil melts and floods the pan when the unit shuts off.
Dirty filter or low refrigerant.Reduced airflow causes freezing, then a flood on thaw.
Disconnected or poorly sloped drain.Water pools at joints instead of leaving the house.

Fixing the equipment itself is a heating and cooling contractor's job. We deal with the water that already got into the building.

What to do right now

  1. Turn off the air conditioner at the thermostat, and at the breaker if water is near the unit.
  2. Place a bucket or towels under active drips.
  3. Photograph the stain, the unit and the pan.
  4. Move belongings away from the wet area.
  5. Call an HVAC contractor for the equipment and call us for the water damage.

Do not poke a hole in a bulging ceiling unless a professional says to. A ceiling filled with water can fall.

Attics, closets and ceilings

Air handlers often sit in attics, closets and crawl spaces, which hides leaks for weeks. Attic insulation soaks and flattens, ceiling drywall below stains and sags, and wood framing stays wet. Crews use meters and a thermal camera to find the damp area and measure how far it reaches.

Ceilings that sag are handled on the ceiling repair page, and a leak that traveled into walls is described on the wall and drywall page. Drying methods are explained at structural drying.

A mold warning that stays factual

Wet drywall and insulation can grow mold if they stay damp, and AC leaks are often slow, which gives mold time. We do not provide mold remediation services, so if you see growth, call a mold professional and keep the area dry. Fast drying of the wet materials reduces the chance of growth.

The EPA advises keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, and ideally between 30 and 50 percent.

Insurance and AC leaks

Sudden overflow from an air conditioner is often covered, while damage from a leak that ran for months can be treated as neglect or maintenance. Policies differ, so ask your insurer. Regular filter changes and an annual service visit reduce your risk and show care if you ever file.

Why attic leaks are expensive

An attic air handler can leak for weeks above a bedroom ceiling with no outward sign. By the time a stain shows, the insulation is soaked, the ceiling board is weak and the joists are wet. Ask a heating and cooling contractor to check the pan and condensate line during seasonal service, and look at the ceiling below the unit each spring.

Standards behind this page

Drying and monitoring on this kind of loss follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration. A separate document, IICRC S520, covers professional mold remediation, which is a different trade that this company does not provide.

FAQ

Questions about AC leak water damage

Can you fix the air conditioner?

No. Equipment repair is for a heating and cooling contractor. This service dries the water damage and repairs the interior surfaces.

Is a small ceiling stain worth a call?

Yes. A small stain can mean wet insulation and framing above. Call before the ceiling sags and ask for a meter reading.

How can I prevent AC leaks?

Change filters on schedule, have the condensate line cleared during service and consider a float switch that shuts the unit off if the pan fills. An HVAC contractor can install one.

Does a window unit leak count?

Yes. A window or portable unit that drains into a wall or floor can cause the same damage.

What if mold appears?

Mold remediation is not offered. Keep the area dry, avoid disturbing growth and contact a mold professional.
