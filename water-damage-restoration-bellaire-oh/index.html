Bellaire grew up around glass, railroads and the Ohio River, and the housing stock shows it. The 2020 census counted 3,870 residents, down from a peak of 15,061 in 1920. Water damage restoration in Bellaire, OH is sent from Colerain Township, about 7 miles away. Call (740) 231-9132 any time.

The Glass City and its old houses

From 1870 to 1885 Bellaire was known as the Glass City, and the Imperial Glass Company opened here in 1904 before closing in 1984. Many homes were built to house workers a century ago. They have plaster walls, wood framing, and plumbing that has been patched many times.

That mix drives the typical call. A supply line fails, a drain backs up, a ceiling stains from an upstairs leak or a roof lets in rain. In a house this old, wet plaster and old paint call for care, and homes built before 1978 may contain lead paint.

Bellaire water department

The Village of Bellaire Water Department supplies water here and operates a water treatment plant. If a main in the street breaks, call the village first. A leak inside the house, past the meter, is the homeowner's side, and that is where a plumber and a restoration crew come in.

Source: Village of Bellaire, village website.

Viaducts, bridges and river lowlands

The town is known for the curved B&O railroad viaduct, which opened in 1871 as the Stone Viaduct Bridge, and for the closed Bellaire Bridge. Streets near the river sit near 709 feet in elevation and can collect runoff. The 1990 Pipe Creek and Wegee Creek flood killed at least 24 people in nearby Belmont County, according to the USGS, and shaped local attitudes about heavy rain.

Insurance and old homes

Older homes sometimes have policies with depreciated payouts, so ask your insurer how it handles plaster, hardwood and matching. Photograph the damage first. Our structural drying page explains the drying records insurers often request.

Plaster, lath and what happens when they get wet

Old Bellaire homes often have plaster over wood lath. Plaster soaks up water and can let go of the lath, so a ceiling that looks fine can fall without warning. Plaster also dries slowly, and crews measure it with meters before deciding whether it can stay.

If plaster is cracked, bulging or damp to the touch, keep people out from under it. A drywall patch over plaster rarely matches, so the finish work in an older home often takes longer than in a newer one.

Heating season in a river town

Older radiators, boilers and supply lines get stressed in cold snaps. A frozen pipe that splits in a wall can run unseen for hours. Learn where the main shutoff is before winter, and open cabinet doors under sinks on cold nights. See frozen pipe damage for the steps to take when a line freezes.
