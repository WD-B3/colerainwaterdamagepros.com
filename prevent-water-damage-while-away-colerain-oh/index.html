Prevent Water Damage While Away from Your Colerain, OH Home

By the Colerain Water Damage Pros team

The worst leaks are the ones nobody is home to catch. A few steps before you leave can prevent most of them.

  • Colerain, OH homeowner guide

A burst pipe on a Saturday afternoon is a nuisance when you are home. If you are in another state for two weeks, it can run the whole time. By the time anyone notices, the floors are soaked and the ceilings below are down. A short checklist before you leave can prevent almost all of that.

This guide is aimed at vacations, winter trips and seasonal homes in Colerain Township and nearby towns.

The best single step: shut off the water

If you are leaving for more than a few days, close the main shutoff and open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the lines. No water pressure means no burst. If you have a water heater, turn it to vacation mode or turn it off, according to the manufacturer's instructions. If you have a sprinkler or well pump, ask a professional how to handle it.

If you are unsure where the valve is, look where the supply line enters the house, near the meter or the water heater. Test the valve before you travel.

Winter: keep the heat on

In freezing weather, turning the heat off can freeze pipes. Set the thermostat to a safe minimum, commonly around 55 degrees Fahrenheit, and open cabinet doors on exterior walls. If you drain the lines, you can leave the heat lower, but ask a plumber about draining the water heater and traps. The frozen pipe page explains what happens when a line freezes.

Add sensors and automation

Water sensors near the water heater, washer, sinks and sump pit send alerts to your phone. Smart shutoff valves close the main when they detect flow that looks like a leak. They cost money, but so does a flooded house. Check your insurer, since some offer discounts for leak detection.

Appliance hoses and old valves

Rubber washer hoses age and burst. Braided stainless hoses last longer. Replace hoses every few years, and check for bulges. Old fixture shutoffs can seize, so test them. The appliance leak page explains how these failures happen.

Weather and the local record

Storms in Belmont County can arrive fast. The 1990 Pipe Creek and Wegee Creek flood came overnight from thunderstorms, according to the U.S. Geological Survey, and the April 2024 flooding caused about $15.7 million in preliminary public damage. If you are away in spring or summer, ask a neighbor to check the basement after heavy rain. Properties near Bridgeport and Bellaire are in creek and river country where that matters most.

Sources: USGS, Belmont County EMA.

Who has a key and a number

Leave a key and a contact sheet with someone who can come quickly. The sheet should list the shutoff location, the plumber's number, your insurer's number and our emergency line. If your neighbor knows what to do, an alert can become a ten-minute problem instead of a ten-day one.

If you come home to water

  1. Do not enter if water is near outlets or the panel.
  2. Close the main shutoff.
  3. Photograph the damage.
  4. Call a restoration crew right away.
  5. Contact your insurer.

The water extraction page explains the first steps.

Second homes, camps and rentals

Seasonal homes and camps are at the highest risk, since they sit empty for months. Drain the system fully if the building will be unheated, and ask a plumber to blow out the lines with air if you want to be thorough. Close all fixture valves and pour non-toxic antifreeze in traps, according to a plumber's guidance.

Rental owners should tell tenants where the main shutoff is and what to do if a pipe bursts. A written one-page sheet taped near the valve works well.

A quick return-home routine

When you come back, open the main slowly and walk the house. Check under sinks, around the water heater and at the washer. Look at ceilings and basement walls for new stains. Run each faucet and check the meter for movement. A fifteen-minute walk-through can catch a slow leak that started while you were gone.

What to tell a house sitter

Write down the shutoff location, the thermostat setting you want, how to check the sump pump and who to call if something goes wrong. Tell the sitter to look at the ceiling in the rooms below any bathroom each visit. A sitter who knows what to look for turns a surprise into a quick call.

Add the restoration number to the list, along with your insurer's claim line and a plumber you trust. Keep the paper copy near the door, since phones and signals fail during storms.

FAQ

Questions about leaving home

Should I turn off the water heater when I leave?

For longer trips, yes, or use vacation mode. Follow the manufacturer's instructions.

Is it safe to leave the heat off in winter?

Not unless the plumbing is drained. Set a safe minimum instead.

What if no one can check the house?

Shut the main, install leak sensors and consider a monitoring service.

Will insurance cover damage while I am away?

Some policies limit coverage for freezing damage if the heat was off. Read your policy before you leave.

Who do I call if a neighbor sees water?

Have them close the main if they can, and call the emergency line. Same-day response when available.
