Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak in Colerain, OH

By the Colerain Water Damage Pros team

Upholstered items hold water like sponges. Whether they can be saved depends on what the water was and how fast you act.

After a leak or flood, the furniture is often the part that hurts most. A mattress is expensive. A sofa may be a favorite. Both soak up water deeply, dry slowly and can hold odor and bacteria for a long time. The decision comes down to a few simple questions.

This guide helps you decide whether to try drying an item or let it go. It is general guidance, not a promise about any one piece of furniture.

Question one: what kind of water was it?

Water comes in categories. Clean water from a supply line or rain through a roof is the least risky. Gray water from a washer or dishwasher has some contamination. Black water from sewage or a flood is heavily contaminated. Upholstery and mattresses that touched black water are discarded in nearly every case, because they cannot be cleaned deep enough.

Water typeMattress or sofa outcome
Clean water, caught quickly.May be saved with fast, careful drying.
Clean water, wet over a day.Risky. Odor and growth may follow.
Gray water.Often discarded, especially if soaked.
Sewage or flood water.Discard.

Question two: how long was it wet?

Time works against you. Within the first day or two, a clean-water spill can sometimes be extracted and dried. After several days, moisture inside foam and padding breeds odor, and drying rarely gets deep enough. If you are not sure how long it sat, assume it sat long enough to be a problem.

Question three: what is it made of?

ItemHow it behaves when wet
Innerspring mattress.Water collects in the padding. Springs can rust.
Memory foam.Soaks up water and dries very slowly.
Upholstered sofa.Padding and webbing hold water, and the frame may swell.
Leather sofa.Can stain and crack, and padding inside stays wet.
Wood or metal frame.Usually survives if cleaned and dried.

If you try to save it

  1. Stop the source of water.
  2. Move the item to a dry, ventilated area, preferably outdoors in dry weather.
  3. Blot and press out as much water as you can, and do not rub.
  4. Use fans and a dehumidifier to dry it.
  5. Check it after a day for odor.

If it still smells musty after a few days of drying, it is time to let it go. Do not use heat to speed it up, since heat can damage foam and set odors.

Document before you discard

Take photos of the item, the label and the wet area. Insurance may cover personal property. Keep a receipt or a note of purchase date and brand. Save a swatch of fabric if the claim needs proof. Do not throw it away until your insurer has had a chance to say what it needs.

Furniture after river and creek flooding

Flood water from the creeks in Belmont County carries silt and sewage. After the April 2024 flooding, homeowners across the region faced the same choice. Upholstered items that touched creek water are discarded, and hard furniture is washed and sanitized. If you live near the creeks, such as in Bridgeport, assume flood-soaked mattresses are a loss. If the water came from a ceiling leak in a hilltop home near Saint Clairsville, saving the item is more realistic.

Source: Belmont County EMA reports on April 2024 flooding.

The room matters too

A wet mattress usually sits on a wet floor. Carpet and pad under a bed need drying, and the same goes for a sofa on hardwood. The carpet page and hardwood page explain what happens under the furniture. Move the furniture off the floor so the area can dry.

Why odor is the real test

Fabric and foam can look dry and still hold moisture deep inside. Smell is the best test. Put your nose near the seam or the underside after the item has been drying for a day or two. A damp or sour smell means moisture is still there, and bacteria are growing. A fresh, neutral smell is a good sign.

Sprays and deodorizers cover odor without removing the cause. If the smell comes back as the room warms or humidity rises, the item is not dry.

Children, allergies and bedding

A mattress that stays damp can trigger allergies and breathing trouble for some people. For a child's bed, be conservative. If there is any doubt about whether the item is clean and dry, replace it.

Bedding, pillows and removable covers can usually be washed and dried on a hot setting, provided the water was clean. Wash anything that touched dirty water separately or discard it.

Salvage or replace: a quick rule

If the water was clean, you caught it within a day and the item is mostly fabric over a hard frame, try to save it. If the water was dirty, the item is foam or thick padding, or it sat for days, replace it. When your insurer will pay for replacement, the choice gets easier.

Standards behind this page

Whether upholstery can be saved comes from the water category in ANSI/IICRC S500, which separates clean, gray and black water. The standard treats porous items that touched Category 3 water as a loss.

FAQ

Questions about wet furniture

Can a wet memory foam mattress be saved?

Rarely. Foam absorbs a lot of water and dries slowly, so odor and growth often follow.

Is it safe to sleep on a mattress that was wet?

Only if it is fully dry and smells clean. When in doubt, replace it.

Does insurance cover a wet mattress?

Often, if the water damage is covered. Personal property coverage varies, so ask your insurer.

Can I clean a sofa that got sewage on it?

No. Sewage-soaked upholstery is discarded. Hard parts of the frame may be sanitized.

How should I dispose of a ruined mattress?

Check with your trash hauler or county solid waste office. Some require a special pickup.
