The damage from a frozen pipe usually appears after the thaw, not during the freeze. Water expands about 9 percent when it turns to ice, and that pressure can split copper, PEX and plastic fittings along a seam or at a joint. Frozen pipe water damage in Colerain, OH starts when the ice melts and the split begins to leak. The 24/7 line at (740) 231-9132 handles the cleanup.

Frozen but not leaking yet: what to do

If a faucet gives only a trickle in cold weather, a pipe may be frozen. Open that faucet so melting water has somewhere to go, and locate your main shutoff before you do anything else.

Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer, a heating pad or warm towels. Never use an open flame, a torch or a space heater near a frozen line. If you see any bulge, crack or wet spot, close the main valve before the ice melts and call us.

When a frozen pipe has already split

Treat a thaw leak as an emergency

Close the main shutoff, open the lowest faucet to drain the lines and turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable. For any electrical or gas concern, call the utility or 911 first.

Photograph the split pipe and every wet surface. Keep the damaged section if you can, because your insurer may ask to see it.

Where pipes freeze in a building

Freezing starts where cold air meets plumbing. That means exterior walls, unheated garages, crawl spaces, attics and runs along the foundation. A kitchen sink on an outside wall and a hose bib shut-off on a basement wall are common weak points.

The water from a split often travels. A break in an exterior wall can soak the insulation, run down to the sill and appear as damp carpet two rooms away. Moisture mapping with a meter finds the whole path.

Insurance and freeze damage

Many homeowners policies cover water damage from a pipe that freezes and bursts, but some require that you kept the home heated. Read the freeze provisions in your policy, ask your insurer what it expects and keep the thermostat records and photos you have.

Standards behind this page

Water released when a frozen line thaws is Category 1 under ANSI/IICRC S500 at first. Dry it quickly, since the same standard allows the category to worsen when water and wet materials sit.

FAQ

Questions about frozen pipes

Do you handle frozen pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Colerain?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day, every day. Winter cold snaps and thaws do not follow business hours, and neither does the line.

Should I thaw the pipe myself?

If the pipe has not split, slow warmth from a hair dryer or warm towels is safe. Never use a flame or a space heater near it. If there is any sign of a crack, close the main valve first.

Why did the leak start after the pipe thawed?

Ice plugs a split pipe. While frozen, the crack cannot leak, and when the ice melts, water pours out through it.

Is the water from a frozen pipe contaminated?

It starts as clean supply water, but it degrades the longer it sits in insulation and framing. Fast drying keeps more materials salvageable.

Will my insurance cover a frozen pipe?

Often, as sudden and accidental damage, but some policies attach conditions about heating the home. Check your policy and ask your insurer.
