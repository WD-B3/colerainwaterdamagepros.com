Water extraction is the removal of standing and trapped water from floors, carpet, pad and low areas with pumps, extraction units and wet vacuums. If water is pooling in your Colerain home or shop, call (740) 231-9132 at any hour. Emergency water removal comes first because every hour water sits, it travels into baseboards, subfloors and the cavities behind walls.

The numbers explain the urgency. One inch of water across a 1,000 square foot floor is about 623 gallons. A half inch across a 400 square foot basement room is still about 125 gallons and more than 1,000 pounds. That much water does not stay put. It wicks into anything porous and keeps spreading while you wait.

When standing water is an emergency

Treat it as an emergency when water is still coming in, when it covers more than one room, when it touches outlets or appliances, or when it came from a drain or a creek. In those cases do not wait for a convenient hour.

Safety comes before everything else

Do not walk through standing water if cords, outlets or appliances are in it. If the electrical panel is dry and reachable, turn off power to the wet area. For any electrical or gas concern, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Never run a household vacuum on water. Stay out of the water entirely if it may have come from a sewer line.

While you wait for the crew, stop the source, lift small items off the floor and photograph the water line on walls and furniture. Those photos matter later if you file a claim.

How water extraction works

Crews begin by finding the water's source and category, because clean supply water, grey water and sewage are handled differently. Then the tool is matched to the depth. Submersible pumps move deep water, truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull water out of carpet and pad, and wet vacuums finish corners and low spots.

Extraction continues until a moisture meter shows that the remaining water is inside materials, not on top of them. Water left inside a wall plate or under a floor cannot be seen, which is why meter readings, not eyesight, close this stage.

Extraction is the start, not the finish

Removing visible water saves a building, but it does not dry one. Wet drywall, framing and subfloors still hold moisture that extraction cannot reach, so structural drying follows with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Soaked carpet is its own decision, explained on the carpet water damage page. Skipping the drying stage is how a dry-looking room turns into a musty one a week later.

Insurance and water removal

Sudden and accidental water, such as a failed supply line, is often covered, and removal is usually part of that claim. Rising water from a creek is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood coverage, which the Ohio Department of Insurance says must be bought separately. Take photos before anything is removed, and ask your insurer what documents it wants.

Water removal in creek country

Colerain Township sits in creek country, with Wheeling Creek running through Blaine and Lansing. When creeks and storm drains run high, low rooms are where water collects, so a basement is usually the first place to check after hard rain.

Standards behind this page

Extraction speed is the first lever in the ANSI/IICRC S500 approach, because every hour that water stays in a building lets it reach more materials. The standard then calls for drying to a measured target, not to a calendar date.

FAQ

Questions about water extraction

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Colerain?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day, every day, and extraction calls are treated as emergencies. Same-day response is offered when available, and the dispatcher explains what to expect on your call.

Is all the water removed in one visit?

Standing water is removed during the first visit. What remains inside materials, such as framing and subfloors, is handled by drying over the following days and confirmed with moisture readings.

Can I use my own wet vacuum?

A shop vacuum rated for wet pickup can handle a small spill. Do not use any vacuum if cords or outlets are in the water, and do not use one on water that may be contaminated.

Will I have to leave the building?

It depends on the water category and where the water is. Clean water from a supply line rarely requires leaving, while sewage or floodwater often does. The dispatcher will say so plainly.

Does extraction include removing wet carpet?

Extraction pulls water out of carpet and pad. Whether the pad or carpet is then removed depends on the water category and how long it was wet, which the assessment decides.
