Where the dispatch route runs

Dispatch is based in Colerain Township in the northeastern part of Belmont County, Ohio. The route reaches the Ohio River towns to the east and south, the county seat to the west and the village of Mount Pleasant to the north.

Colerain Township has about 4,174 residents, covers about 25 square miles and has no incorporated municipalities. Portions of Blaine and Lansing extend into it.

Colerain Township and its villages

The township itself includes the communities of Blaine (ZIP 43909), Lansing (ZIP 43934) and Warnock (ZIP 43967), with ZIP 43916 as the base. Wheeling Creek runs through Blaine and Lansing, which is why runoff and basement water are common calls after hard rain.

Distances below are straight-line estimates from the township, so the drive varies with the route and the traffic. Ask the dispatcher for a realistic estimate when you call.

What the towns have in common and what sets them apart

Martins Ferry is the largest city in Belmont County and sits on the Ohio River across from Wheeling. Bridgeport is a small village at the mouth of Wheeling Creek. Bellaire is an older river village with a long glass-making history. Saint Clairsville is the county seat on I-70 and US 40, and Mount Pleasant is a historic Quaker village in Jefferson County.

Each town has different housing, different water suppliers and, in Mount Pleasant's case, a different county for permits. That is why each has its own page rather than a shared template. The pages explain local conditions and what to do before the crew arrives.

Across all of them, the same rule applies. Stop the water if you can do so safely, stay clear of electrical hazards, photograph the damage and call the line. Same-day response is offered when available.

How the route changes with the weather

Water is rarely the same twice, and the route changes with the weather. After a hard rain, the creek valleys and river bench streets see more calls than the ridgelines. In a cold snap, calls come from every town at once as pipes freeze in outside walls and unheated rooms.

That is why a ZIP code is more useful than a street name when you first call. It tells the dispatcher which crew is closest and which roads are likely to be open. Give your address and the nearest cross street if you are on a rural road, since many township roads share names.

If a road is closed by high water, say so. The dispatcher will tell you what is possible and will not promise a time they cannot keep.

Service is on the Ohio side of the river. We do not work across the river in West Virginia.

How distances are measured

Crews are not stationed in each town. They are dispatched from the Colerain side, which is why each town page lists a straight-line distance and not a promised arrival time. Straight-line numbers are an honest way to compare towns, but your own drive depends on the road, the bridge you cross and the weather.

The river towns share a few bridges and a few main roads, and a closure on one can slow several towns at once. When that happens, the dispatcher will say what they know and will pass your call to the nearest available crew.
