Flood damage cleanup covers water that enters from outside: a creek that overtops its banks, runoff that pours through a door or window, or groundwater that rises through a floor. In Colerain, OH, the 24/7 line at (740) 231-9132 sends crews to remove the water, strip what cannot be saved and dry what can.

Flooding is different from a broken pipe because nothing about the water is clean. Treat every surface it touched as contaminated until it has been cleaned and dried to standard.

If floodwater is in the building now

Stay out of moving or deep water

The National Weather Service slogan applies to roads and basements alike: turn around, do not drown. Do not walk into water you cannot see the bottom of, and never enter a flooded room where outlets or appliances are submerged.

If the electrical panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water, turn off power to the flooded area. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas concern before you call us.

Once it is safe, photograph the high-water mark on walls, lift valuables to upper floors and keep children and pets out of the water. Then call the line and describe how deep it got.

Why floodwater is handled as contaminated

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard generally treats water that rises from rivers, creeks and the ground as Category 3, which means grossly contaminated. It picks up sewage, fuel, fertilizer and soil as it moves, so crews wear protective gear and the cleaning goes beyond drying.

That is also why some materials cannot be kept. Anything porous that sat in Category 3 water is usually removed rather than cleaned.

The cleanup sequence

Remove the water

Pumps and extraction units clear standing water first.

Strip porous materials

Wet pad, insulation and drywall come out.

Clean and sanitize

Surfaces are washed and treated.

Dry and verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach dry standards.

Drying follows the same logic as on the structural drying page, but only after the contaminated materials are gone.

What Belmont County's flood record shows

The U.S. Geological Survey documented the flood of June 14 and 15, 1990 on Pipe Creek and Wegee Creek near Shadyside, in the same county. Violent thunderstorms produced a flood with a recurrence interval above 100 years on Pipe Creek, at least 24 deaths and more than $10 million in property damage.

In April 2024, the county emergency management director estimated about $15.7 million in preliminary damage to public infrastructure, according to The Times Leader. Both events show the same pattern, which is that creek flooding here can build in hours. Know where your valuables are before the next storm, not during it.

Flood insurance is a separate policy

The Ohio Department of Insurance notes that flood insurance is typically not included in homeowners, renters or business policies and has to be bought separately. A waiting period of about 30 days usually applies before new flood coverage begins, so it cannot be arranged after the water arrives.

The department also warns that one inch of floodwater can cause $25,000 of damage to a home. If you are not sure what you have, read your policy or ask your insurer today, and keep photos of every flooded room.

Before you rebuild in a flood hazard area

Belmont County has a floodplain administrator working through the Commissioners' office at 101 West Main Street in Saint Clairsville, along with a flood hazard area development permit. If your property is mapped in a flood hazard area, ask whether a permit applies before you rebuild. You can look up your address on the FEMA flood map to see where it falls.

FAQ

Questions about flood damage cleanup

Do you handle flood damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Colerain?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered around the clock, every day. Flood calls are emergencies, and the dispatcher will go over safety steps with you before anything else.

Is floodwater always considered contaminated?

Under the IICRC S500 standard, water from rivers, creeks and the ground is generally treated as Category 3, meaning grossly contaminated. Clear-looking water can still carry contaminants, so it is handled with protective gear.

Does homeowners insurance cover flooding?

Usually not. Flood is typically excluded from homeowners policies and has to be bought separately, with a waiting period of about 30 days according to the Ohio Department of Insurance. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Can carpet that sat in floodwater be saved?

Almost never. Carpet and pad are porous, and Category 3 water soaks into both, so they are usually removed and replaced.

How do I know if I need a flood hazard permit to rebuild?

Ask the Belmont County floodplain administrator at the Commissioners' office before you start. Whether a permit applies depends on where your property falls on the flood map.
