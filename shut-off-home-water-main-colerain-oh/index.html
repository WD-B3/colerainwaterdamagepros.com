How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Colerain, OH

By the Colerain Water Damage Pros team

When a pipe bursts, every minute counts. The shutoff is the fastest way to limit the damage, and it helps to know where it is before you need it.

Most people have never touched their main shutoff valve. Then a supply line splits, water is running across the floor and the search begins in a panic. This guide helps you find it now, test it and make sure everyone in the house knows where it is.

A shutoff valve is the control that stops water from entering the house. Closing it stops a leak from the supply side, though water already in the pipes can still drain out.

Where to look

The main shutoff is usually where the water line enters the building. In a house with a basement, that is often on the front wall near the water meter or near the water heater. In a slab house, it may be in a utility room, a garage or a closet. In some homes it is outside near the street.

Home typeCommon locations
Basement.Front wall near the meter, or by the water heater.
Crawl space.Where the line enters under the house, sometimes with an access hatch.
Slab.Utility room, garage, or an exterior box.
Mobile or manufactured home.Under the home near the supply, or at an outside box.

Know your valve type

Ball valve

Has a lever handle. A quarter turn so the lever sits crossways to the pipe closes it.

Gate valve

Has a round wheel handle. Turn it clockwise until it stops. Old gate valves can stick or break.

Meter valve

Sits at the water meter. This one often belongs to the utility, so use it only in an emergency.

Curb stop

Sits underground near the street and needs a special key. It belongs to the utility.

How to close it

Find

Locate the valve before you need it.

Turn

Close it fully, clockwise for a wheel and crossways for a lever.

Drain

Open a faucet low in the house to drain what remains.

Open a tap at the lowest point to relieve pressure. Check that the water is off by seeing if the flow stops.

If the valve will not turn

Old valves can seize. Do not force a stuck gate valve with a wrench or pipe, since the stem can snap and make the leak worse. Try turning it a little in both directions to work it loose. If it will not move, close the meter valve if you can reach it, and call the water utility or a plumber right away.

It is wise to have a plumber replace a stuck main valve with a ball valve when things are calm. That repair is cheap compared with a flooded basement.

Fixture shutoffs help too

Most sinks, toilets, washing machines and water heaters have their own shutoff valves. Closing a local valve stops a leak without cutting water to the whole house. Test them once a year, because they can seize from disuse. See the appliance leak page and the water heater page for the usual culprits.

Water systems around Colerain

Different towns have different water suppliers. Martins Ferry runs its own water department and treatment plant, Bellaire has a village water department, and rural parts of Colerain Township may be on a county water system or a private well. If you are on a well, you stop the water by turning off the pump breaker. If you are unsure who supplies you, check your bill. Crews in Martins Ferry and Bellaire hear this question often.

Sources: City of Martins Ferry Water Department, Village of Bellaire.

After the water is off

  1. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel safely.
  2. Take photos of the damage.
  3. Call a plumber to repair the break.
  4. Call a restoration crew to remove the water.
  5. Notify your insurer.

See the burst pipe page for what happens next, and the water extraction page for the cleanup.

Make it a household habit

Tag the valve with a bright label and make sure everyone old enough knows where it is. Keep a flashlight nearby. If you travel in winter, shut the main before leaving. Teach guests and house sitters where it is, and put the plumber's number near the valve.

Test it once a year

A valve that has not been turned in a decade may be stuck, leaking at the stem or broken. Close it gently once a year and open it again. If it leaks around the handle, a plumber can tighten or replace the packing. If it will not turn at all, ask a plumber to replace it before you need it.

While you are there, look at the pipes and the meter for corrosion or drips. A small drip at the main can become a large problem over time.

Standards behind this page

According to the Ohio Department of Insurance, fixing common household leaks prevents costly damage, and the department advises reviewing your coverage with an agent. If water has already spread, ANSI/IICRC S500 guides the drying that follows.

FAQ

Questions about the water main

Will closing the main shutoff damage anything?

No. It stops water from entering the house. You may need to relight a gas water heater pilot later.

What if I cannot find my shutoff?

Check near the meter and the water heater. If you still cannot find it, call the water utility or a plumber.

Do I need to shut the water off when I travel?

In winter or for long trips, it is a good idea. Ask a neighbor to check the house.

Is the meter valve mine to use?

It usually belongs to the utility. Use it only in an emergency and tell the utility.

Does a well house have a main shutoff?

Yes, but you stop the water by turning off the pump breaker and closing the valve after the pressure tank.
