Dark water coming up a drain, and the room smells?

If a floor drain, tub or toilet is pushing up dark or foul water, you have a sewage backup. The trade name for it is Category 3 water, which the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard defines as grossly contaminated. It can carry bacteria and viruses, so it is handled with protective gear, not a mop.

Sewage backup cleanup in Colerain, OH starts with the 24/7 line at (740) 231-9132. Keep people and pets out of the room and stop using water in the house until the backup is cleared.

Sewage backup: what to do first

Keep out of the water

Do not touch or wade through sewage. Do not use sinks, tubs or toilets, because anything you flush or drain may add to the backup. Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system away.

If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 about the electrical risk before you call us.

  • Open windows if the odor is strong and it is safe.
  • Photograph the backup and the water line before cleanup.
  • Call a plumber or the sewer authority to clear the blockage. Cleanup cannot hold if the line backs up again.

Why sewage backs up

CauseWhere the problem sits
Blocked main line from roots or grease.Your private lateral, usually the homeowner's responsibility.
Collapsed or cracked line.Your lateral, or the shared line to the street.
Municipal main overwhelmed in heavy rain.The public sewer. Report it to the village, city or sewer district.
Failed sump or sewage ejector pump.Equipment in your basement.

If neighbors are also backing up, tell the sewer authority at once. In Belmont County, which office to call depends on where you live, so ask your village or city office.

How contaminated water is cleaned

Contain

Close the area and protect clean rooms.

Remove

Water is extracted and contaminated porous materials are bagged.

Sanitize

Surfaces are washed and treated with disinfectant.

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry what remains.

Porous materials that sat in Category 3 water, such as carpet, pad, insulation and wet drywall, are usually removed and discarded. Hard surfaces like concrete and tile are cleaned and disinfected. Crews wear gloves, boots, eye protection and respirators.

Insurance and sewer backups

Standard homeowners policies frequently exclude water that backs up through drains or sumps unless you added a water backup endorsement. Flood from a creek is a different exclusion. Check your policy for the words sewer or drain backup, ask your insurer and photograph everything before cleanup.

The flood damage page covers outside water that rises into a building, and the basement page covers the room where backups usually land.

Health precautions while you wait

Wash hands with soap and clean water after any contact with the affected area, and keep food and dishes away from it. The CDC advises that anyone with cuts or open sores avoid contact with floodwater and sewage, and that children and people with weakened immune systems stay clear of the cleanup zone.

Wear waterproof gloves, boots and eye protection if you must enter the room, and change out of those clothes before moving through the rest of the house. Keep pets out. If anyone develops stomach illness or skin irritation after exposure, contact a doctor and mention the sewage.

FAQ

Questions about sewage backups

Do you handle sewage backup emergencies at night or on weekends in Colerain?

Yes. Sewage calls are treated as emergencies, and the dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day, every day. Keep everyone out of the contaminated area while you wait.

Is it safe to clean up sewage myself?

Small, contained spills can be handled with gloves, boots and eye protection, but a real backup carries health risks. Category 3 water needs protective gear and disinfection, so most homeowners call a crew.

What if my carpet only got a little wet?

Carpet and pad that touched sewage are usually discarded no matter how small the area, because they absorb contamination that cleaning cannot reach.

Who fixes the sewer line?

A plumber clears or repairs your private line. If the public main is the problem, the village, city or sewer district handles it. Cleanup is a separate job.

Will insurance cover a sewage backup?

Only if your policy has water backup coverage. Many standard policies exclude it. Check your policy for backup language and ask your insurer.
