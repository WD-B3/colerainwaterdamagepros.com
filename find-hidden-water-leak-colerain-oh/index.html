How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Colerain, OH

By the Colerain Water Damage Pros team

A hidden leak shows up as a high water bill, a musty smell or a stain with no obvious source. Here is how to narrow it down.

Hidden leaks cause some of the worst water damage because nobody sees them. They run inside walls, under floors and above ceilings for weeks. By the time a stain appears, the damage is often well along. This guide shows how to look for a leak yourself and when to call for help.

The Ohio Department of Insurance notes that the average Ohio family can waste nearly 180 gallons of water per week from household leaks, which shows how common slow leaks are.

Clues that a leak is hiding

  • A water bill that rises without a change in use.
  • The sound of running water when everything is off.
  • A water meter that keeps moving when no one is using water.
  • A musty smell with no obvious source.
  • Stains, bubbling paint or warped flooring.
  • A warm spot on a floor, which can point to a hot water line.
  • Low water pressure.

The water meter test

Shut off

Turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water, including the ice maker.

Read

Write down the meter reading or note the position of the dial.

Wait

Wait an hour or two without using water, then read it again.

If the meter moved, water is going somewhere. Many meters also have a small leak indicator that spins when water flows. If the meter did not move, the leak may be only when fixtures are running, such as a shower valve.

The toilet dye test

Toilets leak silently from the tank into the bowl. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait about fifteen minutes without flushing, and look in the bowl. If color shows up, the flapper is leaking. It is a cheap fix that can waste a lot of water.

Leaks under concrete

A leak in a pressurized line under a slab is hard to find without special equipment. Signs include a warm floor, a constantly running meter and damp carpet. A plumber uses acoustic, thermal or pressure methods to locate it. The slab leak page explains who does what.

Finding leaks in older Belmont County houses

Older plumbing fails at fittings, corroded sections and old shutoff valves. Many homes around Mount Pleasant and Martins Ferry have original pipes in plaster walls, which makes leaks harder to see. Rural properties on private wells may notice a pump that cycles too often, which can point to a leak after the pressure tank. If you are on a municipal supply, check the meter. If you are on a well, listen to the pump.

Source: Ohio Department of Insurance on household leaks, local plumbing practice.

Tools that help

Moisture meters and thermal cameras are the professional tools. Inexpensive leak sensors that sit on the floor near water heaters and under sinks send an alert to a phone. They are cheap insurance. A restoration crew can read your walls and floors and tell you where the water has traveled, though a plumber must find and fix the source. See the structural drying page.

What to do when you find it

  1. Shut off water to the leaking fixture or the main.
  2. Photograph the leak and the damage.
  3. Call a plumber to repair it.
  4. Call a restoration crew to dry the area.
  5. Tell your insurer.

Outdoor and irrigation leaks

Not all hidden leaks are inside. A leak in the line between the meter and the house shows up as a soggy patch in the yard, a greener strip of grass or a damp foundation. Outdoor spigots and irrigation lines can leak for weeks. If your meter moves and everything inside is off, walk the line from the meter to the house and look for soft ground.

Wet soil next to a foundation can also push water into a basement. See the basement page for what to do if the water reaches the house.

When to call a pro right away

Call a plumber if the meter moves with everything off, if you hear running water in the walls or if you see a stain growing. Call a restoration crew if the materials are wet. Delay turns a repair into a rebuild. A small leak found early may cost the price of a fitting. A large one found late can cost a floor.

Why quick action pays

A leak that wets the same patch of wall for a month can rot the bottom plate and stain the ceiling below. Finding it in week one means a fitting. Finding it in week eight can mean framing and finishes. If you suspect a leak and cannot find it, do not wait for a stain. Ask a plumber to test the system and ask for a moisture reading of the suspect area.

FAQ

Questions about hidden leaks

How do I know if the leak is in the wall?

A stain, a moving meter and a musty smell with no visible source suggest a leak inside the wall. A moisture reading can confirm it.

Can I use a smart water monitor?

Yes. Some units watch flow at the main and shut off the valve if something looks wrong. A plumber can install one.

Is a high water bill always a leak?

No. Seasonal use and rate changes matter. But a sudden rise with no change in habits is a clue.

What if the meter does not move but I smell mustiness?

The moisture may come from condensation, groundwater or a roof leak. A reading helps sort out the source.

Do I need a plumber or a restoration company?

Both, in order. The plumber stops and fixes the leak, and the restoration crew dries the damage.
