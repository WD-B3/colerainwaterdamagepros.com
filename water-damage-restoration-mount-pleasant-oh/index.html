A different county, a different office

Mount Pleasant sits in Jefferson County, so building permits, floodplain questions and records go through Jefferson County and the village, not Belmont County. If a repair needs a permit, ask the village or county offices that apply to Mount Pleasant. The ZIP code is 43939.

Insurers do not care about county lines, but local offices do. Keep the permit contact straight, and tell the dispatcher that you are in Jefferson County.

A National Historic Landmark village

Quakers laid the village out in 1803, and most of it is now a National Historic Landmark district. The Benjamin Lundy House is a National Historic Landmark in its own right, and the antislavery newspapers The Philanthropist and The Genius of Universal Emancipation were published here in the early nineteenth century.

In an old district, the repair job must respect the building. Original plaster, wide-plank floors and trim cannot always be replaced like new. Crews dry in place when they can and avoid removing historic material without a reason. Ask the village if exterior changes need review.

High ground, but not dry

The village sits at about 1,217 feet, well above the river towns, so river flooding is not the main worry. Roof leaks, aging plumbing, water heaters and foundation seepage after storms are. Heavy rain in the region, like the storms that caused the 1990 Pipe Creek flood, still finds its way into old stone and brick basements.

Stone, brick and old basements

Many village homes sit on stone or brick foundations laid by hand. Mortar joints open over time, and water finds the gaps after heavy rain. The wall may stay damp for days while a damp smell builds, which is why a meter is more reliable than sight.

Drying a stone basement takes patience. Dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air and stone, and crews avoid blasting hot air that can crack old mortar. The structural drying page explains the method.

Winter in the hills

The village sits on high ground, and cold arrives earlier here than in the river towns. A frozen line in an unheated porch or crawl space can split and flood a floor when it thaws. Know your main shutoff and keep the heat on in unoccupied buildings. If a line does freeze, close the main before thawing it.
